\textbf{Source and sample.} Daily prices from Yahoo Finance, retrieved through the \texttt{yfinance} API for the six ETFs,
AGG, the S\&P 500 total return index (\texttt{\textasciicircum SP500TR}) and the 13-week T-bill yield (\texttt{\textasciicircum IRX}).
Data start on \res{data_start} and end on \res{data_end}. The youngest ETF (DBC, launched in February 2006) and the 252-day
risk window set the first decision date to \res{first_decision}, so the backtest covers \res{sample_months} monthly returns
from \res{sample_start} to \res{sample_end}, including the global financial crisis, the Covid crash and the 2022 rate shock.

\textbf{Adjustments and calculations.} Prices are adjusted for splits and distributions, so returns are total returns
after fund fees. Month-end values are the last close of each month; returns are simple returns, so that portfolio returns
are exact weighted sums. The cash return in month $t$ is the \texttt{\textasciicircum IRX} yield at the end of month $t-1$
divided by 12 (on average \res{rf_mean} p.a.). Averages are annualised by $\times 12$ and volatilities by $\times\sqrt{12}$.
The Sharpe ratio uses excess returns over T-bills. Beta and Jensen's alpha come from a regression of excess returns on the
market's (S\&P 500 total return) excess returns; beta is \emph{not} annualised, alpha is $\times 12$, and its $t$-statistic
uses Newey-West standard errors \citep{newey1987}. The information ratio is the annualised mean return in excess of the
60/40 benchmark divided by the tracking error. Trading costs of \res{cost_bps} are charged on every unit of turnover
(the sum of absolute weight changes against drifted weights) for the strategy and the benchmark alike. Every number in
this prospectus is computed in the notebook; clicking a value opens the notebook section that computes it.
